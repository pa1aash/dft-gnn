**Figure 1.** The models compared in this work. Every graph network starts from the pristine host supercell of the reference calculation, in which the oxygen atom to be removed (orange) stays in place and carries a binary vacancy flag; the relaxed defect geometry is never used, because it is unknown at deployment. The supercell becomes a periodic radius graph with a 5.0 Å cutoff (nodes: learned element embedding plus the vacancy flag; edges: Gaussian expansion of the interatomic distance) and is read by a MEGNet backbone. **S** (blue) predicts the neutral oxygen-vacancy formation energy $E_\mathrm{f}$ from the structure alone. **D** (green) receives the same graph plus the DFT descriptors of the pristine host: the host-electronic class is appended to the initial global state $u^{(0)}$ and the site-electronic class to the initial features $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{v}^{(0)}$ of the vacancy node (D-state injection). **P** (purple) is staged: P1, with the backbone and readout of S, predicts the standardised D descriptors from the structure, and D, trained on true descriptors, maps the structure and these predicted descriptors to $E_\mathrm{f}$; P against D on the same test hosts isolates the cost of predicting the descriptors. The non-network baselines are a random forest on the 70 Kumagai descriptors (**RF**, orange) and the physics floor **B0** (grey), a linear model with intercept in two host descriptors, oxide stability and the PBEsol(+U) band gap. The solid, filled box is the primary backbone (v2: He-uniform initialisation, zero biases), adopted after diagnostics of the originally registered backbone; a dashed, open outline offset behind it marks the originally registered backbone (v1) of the same model, which is otherwise identical. Colours are those of every results figure.
