\subsection{Host and site contributions to the formation energy}
\label{sec:host_site}

The first question is how much of the variation of the neutral oxygen-vacancy formation energy $E_\mathrm{f}$ is set by the host oxide and how much by the individual oxygen site. Grouped by host, the intraclass correlation is $\mathrm{ICC}_\mathrm{host} = \numVarianceDecompositionHostIcc{}$ [\numVarianceDecompositionHostIccCiLo{}, \numVarianceDecompositionHostIccCiHi{}], with a between-host variance component of \numVarianceDecompositionHostSigmaTwoBetween{}~\eVsq{} against a within-host component of \numVarianceDecompositionHostSigmaTwoWithin{}~\eVsq{} (Section~\ref{sec:data}). The energy needed to remove a neutral oxygen atom is therefore governed mainly by the oxide, through the stability of its lattice and the position of the band edges that receive the two electrons, and only secondarily by the coordination of the site. A total site-level MAE thus measures mostly how well a model has learned oxide stability, which is why the within-host residual MAE, which removes each host's mean (Section~\ref{sec:statistics}), is reported beside it throughout.

Host-level information alone bounds what can be achieved. The host-mean oracle, which assigns every site the true mean $E_\mathrm{f}$ of its host, reaches a site MAE of \numHostMeanOracleAggregateMaeMean{} [\numHostMeanOracleAggregateMaeCiLo{}, \numHostMeanOracleAggregateMaeCiHi{}]~eV, and any predictor that is constant within a host has a within-host residual MAE of \numHostMeanOracleAggregateWithinHostMaeMean{} [\numHostMeanOracleAggregateWithinHostMaeCiLo{}, \numHostMeanOracleAggregateWithinHostMaeCiHi{}]~eV, the zero-skill value. The thermodynamic floor B0, linear in the oxide formation energy and the band gap, gives \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetBZeroMaeMean{} [\numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetBZeroMaeCiLo{}, \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetBZeroMaeCiHi{}]~eV with \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} training hosts and \numGOnePrimaryDescriptiveCurvesMaeBZeroTwentyFiveMean{}~eV with \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemOne{}: two pristine-host quantities place hosts to within roughly three quarters of an electronvolt and gain little from more training oxides.

The random forests on the descriptors of Kumagai et al.\ \citep{Kumagai2021insights} close much of the gap to the oracle. With \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} training hosts, RF-Kumagai reaches \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfKumagaiMaeMean{} [\numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfKumagaiMaeCiLo{}, \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfKumagaiMaeCiHi{}]~eV, within \numBaselinesSummaryRfKumagaiVsPublishedDifferenceEV{}~eV of the published \numBaselinesSummaryRfKumagaiVsPublishedPublishedEV{}~eV, and also resolves part of the site-to-site variation, with a within-host residual MAE of \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfKumagaiWithinHostMaeMean{} [\numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfKumagaiWithinHostMaeCiLo{}, \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfKumagaiWithinHostMaeCiHi{}]~eV. What is established is the scale of the problem: the target is dominated by host thermodynamics, every model is judged first on how it places hosts, and site resolution must be read from the within-host metric.

\subsection{Reproduction of the published structure-only accuracy}
\label{sec:reproduction}

We first checked that the structure-only network S reaches the published accuracy of a structure-only model for this target (claim C0). On the host split of Kiyohara et al.\ \citep{Kiyohara2025machinelearning}, with \numFilterCascadeKiyoharaSplitHostsTrain{} training, \numFilterCascadeKiyoharaSplitHostsVal{} validation and \numFilterCascadeKiyoharaSplitHostsTest{} test hosts, the seed-ensemble S of v1 reaches \numCZeroOfficialSEnsembleMaePoint{} [\numCZeroOfficialSEnsembleMaeCiLo{}, \numCZeroOfficialSEnsembleMaeCiHi{}]~eV (host-cluster bootstrap), against the published \numCZeroOfficialComparisonEVKiyoharaPublished{}~eV and below the registered gate of \numCZeroOfficialCZeroThresholdEV{}~eV. RF-Kumagai on the same split gives \numCZeroOfficialRfKumagaiSameSplitMaeEVPoint{} [\numCZeroOfficialRfKumagaiSameSplitMaeEVCiLo{}, \numCZeroOfficialRfKumagaiSameSplitMaeEVCiHi{}]~eV. On our formula-grouped protocol with \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} training hosts, S reaches \numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetSixHundredFiftyFourMAESMean{} [\numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetSixHundredFiftyFourMAESCiLo{}, \numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetSixHundredFiftyFourMAESCiHi{}]~eV on v1 and \numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourSMaeMean{} [\numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourSMaeCiLo{}, \numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourSMaeCiHi{}]~eV on v2 (post hoc).

Two qualifications apply. The hyperparameters were tuned on one resample of our protocol, and \numCZeroOfficialTuningOverlapWithKiyoharaTestSixHundredFiftyFourInKiyoharaTest{} of the Kiyohara test hosts lie in the largest tuning anchor, \numCZeroOfficialTuningOverlapWithKiyoharaTestSixHundredFiftyFourOfWhichValidationHosts{} of them among its validation hosts, so that comparison is not fully held out at the level of the hyperparameters, although the weights never saw those hosts. C0 on the Kiyohara split exists for v1 only.

The reported quantity is the seed ensemble, the mean prediction of networks trained from three sets of random starting weights. At \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} training hosts on v1, single-network values of $A$ scatter with a standard deviation of \numGOneVarianceAdvantageSixHundredFiftyFourSingleSeedASdOverAllThirty{}~eV, above $\delta$, whereas the ensemble value varies between resamples by \numGOneVarianceAdvantageSixHundredFiftyFourEnsembleASdOverResamples{}~eV, so a comparison of single networks could not resolve the tolerance. This establishes that S reproduces the published structure-only accuracy on both protocols; it does not rank S against the published network.

For a practitioner the measurements support a bounded statement: for deep, localised neutral oxygen vacancies in non-magnetic ordered oxides (Section~\ref{sec:domain}), a structure-only network trained on \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} or more host oxides predicts $E_\mathrm{f}$ on unseen oxides with a site error within $\delta$ of the same network given the pristine-host DFT electronic descriptors, on both networks and up to a mean of \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTwenty{} training hosts. Descriptor-free prediction therefore suffices within the stated tolerance at these data sizes, for this domain and for test oxides from the chemical families of the training set. It is not supported at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFive{} hosts or fewer, beyond the largest cross-validation training size, for cation families absent from training on the primary network, for shallow-donor oxides, or for other architectures than the one cross-checked in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivities}. Nor do the measurements show that the descriptors carry no information about the vacancy: on the primary network they lower the error by an amount whose interval excludes zero but lies below the tolerance. Why the advantage shrinks with data, and why the networks disagree in its sign, is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Site resolution within a host}
\label{sec:within_host}

The local chemistry of the vacancy shows only in how well a model distinguishes the oxygen sites of one oxide. Figure~\ref{fig:within_host}a shows the within-host skill, the zero-skill value minus the model's within-host residual MAE. On v1, S has no within-host skill up to \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFour{} training hosts: its residual, \numGOneWithinHostWithinHostResidualMaePerBudgetTwoHundredSMean{}~eV, equals the zero-skill value, because it gave nearly the same prediction for every site of a host (Section~\ref{sec:backbones}). At \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} hosts the within-host residual MAE is \numGOneWithinHostWithinHostResidualMaePerBudgetSixHundredFiftyFourSMean{} (S) and \numGOneWithinHostWithinHostResidualMaePerBudgetSixHundredFiftyFourDMean{}~eV (D) on v1 and \numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourSWithinHostMaeMean{} and \numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourDStateWithinHostMaeMean{}~eV on v2: every model is below the zero-skill value \numHostMeanOracleAggregateWithinHostMaeMean{}~eV, but each removes only a modest part of the site-to-site spread.

\subsection{Staged prediction through descriptors}
\label{sec:staging}

The staged model P asks how much of the value of the DFT descriptors survives when they are predicted from the structure rather than computed (claim C3a). It matters because below \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} training hosts their advantage is not bounded below $\delta$ under the registered rule, yet each new oxide needs a DFT calculation of its pristine host. In P, P1 predicts the \numRfElectronicNFeatures{} descriptors from the pristine structure, and D of the same resample, budget and seed receives them in place of the true ones (Figure~\ref{fig:staging}).
