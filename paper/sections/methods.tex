\section{Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

The study asks how much a graph network that sees only the pristine crystal structure of an oxide, with the oxygen site to be removed marked, loses against the same network given the electronic structure of the pristine host and site from density-functional theory (DFT), and how this gap changes as the number of training hosts grows. Every model predicts the same physical quantity, the formation energy of the neutral oxygen vacancy, and every comparison is made on the same held-out hosts.

\subsection{Graph Representation}
\label{sec:graph}

Each example is the pristine host supercell of the reference calculation, taken from the release (\numGraphsVOneStatsAtomsMin{} to \numGraphsVOneStatsAtomsMax{} atoms, median \numGraphsVOneStatsAtomsMedian{}). The oxygen atom to be removed stays in the graph and carries a binary vacancy flag in its node features (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}b). The graph is periodic, with an edge between every pair of atoms closer than \numCfgGraphCutoffA{}~\AA{}. Node features are a learned embedding of the element plus the vacancy flag; edge features are a Gaussian expansion of the interatomic distance with the centres and width of MEGNet \citep{Chen2019graph}; the global state starts as a vector of dimension \numGraphsVOneStateDim{}.

The relaxed defect cell is deliberately not used. When the oxygen atom is removed, the neighbouring cations relax in response to the two electrons left behind, and that relaxation is an output of the very defect calculation a model is meant to replace: at deployment the site to be removed is known, but the relaxed defect geometry is not. A model given the relaxed cell would read part of the answer from its input. The perturbation is small but informative; in wurtzite ZnO, for example, no atom of the release's relaxed neutral defect cell lies more than \numStructureAuditZnoPristineVsRelaxedDisplacementAMaxOfMatchedAtoms{}~\AA{} from its pristine position. Because the pristine supercell is the supercell of the DFT defect calculation, the periodic images of the vacancy are separated exactly as in the reference energies.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:models}

\subsubsection{Baselines}

The physics floor B0 is a linear model with intercept in \numCfgModelsPhysicsFloorNParameters{} host descriptors, the oxide formation energy per atom and the PBEsol(+$U$) band gap. These are the two quantities that earlier work identified as the main thermodynamic and electronic controls of oxygen-vacancy formation \citep{Deml2014oxide,Deml2015intrinsic}: the formation energy measures how strongly oxygen is bound, and the band gap the cost of placing the two released electrons. B0 is constant within a host by construction. Three random forests \citep{Breiman2001random,Pedregosa2018scikitlearn} use \numRfKumagaiHyperparametersNEstimators{} trees and the feature-subset size selected by the search released by Kumagai et al.\ \citep{Kumagai2021insights}: RF-Kumagai on all \numRfKumagaiNFeatures{} descriptors, RF-electronic on the \numRfElectronicNFeatures{} electronic descriptors and RF-structural on the \numRfStructuralNFeatures{} structural and compositional descriptors. The baselines are fitted on the same training hosts as the networks and are reported descriptively.

\subsubsection{Graph networks}

All graph networks share a backbone of MEGNet blocks \citep{Chen2019graph} from matgl \vermatgl{} \citep{Ko2025materials}, in its PyTorch Geometric implementation \citep{Fey2019fast,Paszke2019pytorch}. The structure-only model S reads out the concatenation of three vectors: the final embedding of the vacancy node, a pooling of all node embeddings (Set2Set \citep{Vinyals2016order} or the mean, a tuned choice) and the final global state; a multilayer perceptron maps this vector to $E_\mathrm{f}$. The readout mirrors the variance structure of the target (Section~\ref{sec:data}): the pooled embedding and the global state carry the host-level thermodynamic term, and the vacancy-node embedding carries the local environment of the site. A diagram of the readout is given in the Supplementary Materials.

The descriptor model D is S plus the two electronic descriptor classes, standardised with training-set statistics. Two injection routes were defined before training. In D-state the host-electronic descriptors are appended to the initial global state and the site-electronic descriptors to the initial features of the vacancy node, so that each class enters where its physics acts; in D-late all descriptors are concatenated to the readout vector. The pre-specified rule kept the route with the lower mean, over the tuning anchors, of the best validation error. D-state was selected, with a mean of \numTuningSummaryDecisionMeansEVDState{}~eV against \numTuningSummaryDecisionMeansEVDLate{}~eV for D-late; the two routes are indistinguishable on this objective, and D-late trained in the full protocol at the three largest budgets is reported as an extension.

The descriptor predictor P1 has the backbone and readout of S with a multi-output head that predicts all \numRfElectronicNFeatures{} standardised descriptors of D, trained with a mean-squared-error loss with equal weights. The staged model P is the composition of P1 and D: at inference, D receives the descriptors predicted by P1 instead of the true ones, on the same graph. D is trained on true descriptors, as in serial pipelines that first predict a charge descriptor and then the energy \citep{Linton2026framework}. The P1 and D checkpoints of the same resample, budget and seed are composed, so D acts as the true-descriptor swap-in for P, and the difference between them isolates the cost of predicting rather than knowing the electronic structure. The staged model of v1 is a registered analysis; P-v2 is post hoc.

\subsubsection{Backbones: v2 primary, v1 as originally registered}
\label{sec:backbones}

Two backbones are reported. The primary backbone of this paper, v2, is the MEGNet backbone above with He-uniform initialisation of the weights of every linear layer and zero biases \citep{He2015delving}. The originally registered backbone, v1, uses the default initialisation of the library. Everything else, including the graphs, readout, training protocol, search space and splits, is identical.

The analysis plan registered before any network was trained used backbone v1 (tag \texttt{prereg-v1}, commit \commitprereg{}, \dateprereg{}); the tuned v1 values were frozen as tag \texttt{tuned-v1} (\datetunedvone{}). Diagnostics of v1, the learning-curve analysis (tag \texttt{g1-v1}, \dategone{}) and a site-resolution check (commit \commitsiteres{}, \datesiteres{}), showed that the structure-only model gave nearly the same prediction for every oxygen site of a host at budgets up to \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{} hosts. At \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{} training hosts, the median standard deviation of its predictions across the labelled sites of a test host was \numSSiteResolutionForensicCheckpointsSTwoHundredFlagSweepTrainedMedianSdLabelled{}~eV, against a target spread of \numSSiteResolutionForensicCheckpointsSTwoHundredFlagSweepTrainedMedianTargetSd{}~eV. The check found no implementation error: with the default initialisation, inequivalent oxygen sites of one host give outputs that differ only at the level of \numSSiteResolutionForensicCheckpointsSTwoHundredFlagSweepInitMedianSdLabelled{}~eV before training, and at small budgets training never created the difference. These diagnostics were computed on the test-host predictions of v1.

The authors then adopted a modified backbone, v2, selected by a validation-host-only design screen under a protocol logged in the deviations file before any v2 production run (commit \commitvtwoprotocol{}, \datevtwoprotocol{}). The screen trained S with four variants (the registered backbone, He initialisation, an additional local readout of the bonds of the vacancy node, and both changes) at the three tuning anchors of resample~\numCfgTuningAnchorResample{} with three seeds, with the test hosts never loaded. The logged rule kept the variant with the lowest mean validation error over the anchors, which was He initialisation alone (recorded \datevtwoselect{}). S-v2 and D-v2 were then re-tuned with the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:tuning}, and their values were frozen (commit \commitvtwofreeze{}, \datevtwofreeze{}) before the first v2 production run (\datevtwofirstrun{}). Test hosts were not used in the screen or the re-tuning. P1-v2 uses the tuned P1 values of v1 with the v2 initialisation; it was not re-tuned.

v2 is the primary backbone, designated by the author on \dateauthordecision{}; the registered v1 results are reported alongside, in full. Because the decision to build a second backbone followed diagnostics of v1 on test hosts, every v2 result is labelled post hoc (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).

\subsection{Training and Hyperparameter Tuning}
\label{sec:training}

\subsubsection{Training protocol}

The loss is the L1 error on $E_\mathrm{f}$ standardised with the training-set mean and standard deviation. The optimiser is AdamW \citep{Loshchilov2019decoupled} with a ReduceLROnPlateau schedule (factor \numCfgTrainingSchedulerFactor{}, patience \numCfgTrainingSchedulerPatience{} epochs). A validation set, a fraction \numCfgTrainingValidationFrac{} of the training hosts and at least \numCfgTrainingValidationMinHosts{} of them, is carved out of every training set, with its seed derived by a hash from the resample, the budget and the training seed. A training budget $B$ counts these validation hosts, so $B$ is the number of hosts with DFT labels that a model consumes. Early stopping monitors the validation error with a patience of \numCfgTrainingEarlyStoppingPatience{} epochs and restores the weights of the best epoch. Every resample and budget is trained with three seeds, and the prediction of a model at a test site is the arithmetic mean of its three seed predictions (the seed ensemble).

\subsubsection{Epoch cap}

The maximum number of epochs was fixed by a rule logged before the calibration pilot ran: the cap is the smallest multiple of fifty at or above one and a half times the slowest convergence epoch over the piloted budgets, and the patience is the larger of thirty epochs and fifteen per cent of the cap. In the pilot the slowest run converged at epoch \numPilotEpochsMaxOverBudgetsEConv{}, which gave a cap of \numCfgTrainingMaxEpochs{} epochs and a patience of \numPilotEpochsPatience{}, used for both backbones in tuning and in every later run. Its effect was measured by a registered sensitivity analysis: S and D at $B = \numCfgSensitivityEpochCapBudget{}$ on the first three resamples were retrained with a cap of \numCfgSensitivityEpochCapMaxEpochs{} epochs, with the advantage declared cap-insensitive if it changed by at most \numCfgSensitivityEpochCapCapInsensitiveIfAbsDeltaALeEV{}~eV. The advantage was identical under both caps (difference \numGOneCapSensitivityASixHundredMinusATwoHundredMean{}~eV). The cap nonetheless stopped some production runs: \numIngestMOneRecomputeEpochCapOursSSixHundredFiftyFourCappedOfThirty{} of the \numCappedRunAuditCountsSweepSSixHundredFiftyFourTotal{} v1 runs of S at the largest budget, and \numIngestMOneRecomputeEpochCapHisRunsAtCapVTwoSweepAtCap{} of the \numIngestMOneRecomputeEpochCapHisRunsAtCapVTwoSweepRuns{} runs of the v2 sweep, ran all \numCfgTrainingMaxEpochs{} epochs. A post hoc rerun of every capped v1 run with the longer cap was specified before any of its results existed and is reported only as a sensitivity analysis.

\subsection{Data Splits}
\label{sec:splits}

Every split groups by host, so no host contributes sites to both training and test; the designs are drawn in the Supplementary Materials.

\textit{Grouped hold-out with nested budgets (primary design; registered for v1).} Each of \numCfgSplitNOuterResamples{} outer resamples holds out \numSplitMetaNTestHosts{} hosts (a fraction \numCfgSplitTestFraction{}) as test hosts, stratified by quintiles of the host-mean $E_\mathrm{f}$, and orders the remaining pool of \numSplitMetaPoolSize{} hosts so that the training set at budget $B$ is the first $B$ hosts of that order. The budgets are \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemOne{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemTwo{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFive{} and \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemSix{} hosts, nested within a resample, and the test hosts of a resample are the same at every budget. Every learning curve is drawn against $B$. Independent hold-outs leave \numSplitMetaCoverageNeverInTest{} hosts never tested; they are excluded from every per-host analysis.

\textit{Published split (registered for v1).} On the split released by Kiyohara et al.\ \citep{Kiyohara2025machinelearning}, the networks train on its \numFilterCascadeKiyoharaSplitHostsTrain{} training hosts, stop early on its \numFilterCascadeKiyoharaSplitHostsVal{} validation hosts and are tested on its \numFilterCascadeKiyoharaSplitHostsTest{} test hosts, with the values of the largest anchor. This places the structure-only model against the published structure-only accuracy for neutral oxygen vacancies.

\textit{Grouped $K$-fold cross-validation (extension).} The outer resamples cap training at \numSplitMetaPoolSize{} hosts and leave some hosts untested. With $K = \numSplitCvKsItemOne{}$ and $K = \numSplitCvKsItemTwo{}$ folds over all \numSplitCvNHosts{} hosts, every host is tested exactly once and each fold trains on every host outside it. Folds are assigned by a seeded stratified round robin over the host-mean $E_\mathrm{f}$ quintiles (seed \numSplitCvSeed{}). S and D are trained with three seeds on both backbones, with the values of the largest anchor.

\textit{Additional resamples and seeds (extension).} To narrow the resample- and seed-level spread at the two largest budgets, the same split engine draws twenty further hold-outs of \numSplitExtNTestHosts{} test hosts, and S and D are trained with five seeds at $B = \numSplitExtBudgetsItemFive{}$ and $B = \numSplitExtBudgetsItemSix{}$ on both backbones. The registered ten-resample, three-seed result is reported beside it and is never replaced.

\textit{Leave one chemistry out (registered for v1).} To test transfer to cation chemistries absent from training, hosts are grouped into \numSplitLocoNFamilies{} families by the set of periodic-table groups of their cations (lanthanides and actinides counted in group three), and grouped $k$-fold cross-validation with $k = \numCfgRobustnessLocoK{}$ holds out whole families. Each fold trains on every host outside it, with the values of the largest anchor and three seeds; the v1 runs carve their validation hosts with the fold index as the resample seed.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:statistics}

\textit{Metrics.} The primary metric is the site-level test mean absolute error (MAE) in eV. Because $\mathrm{ICC}_\mathrm{host} = \numVarianceDecompositionHostIcc{}$, a site-level MAE is governed mostly by how well a model places each host, and it can hide whether the model distinguishes the oxygen sites of one host, which is where the local defect chemistry acts. Two secondary metrics separate the two scales. The within-host residual MAE removes each host's mean: for a test host with at least two sites, the residual at site $j$ is the prediction minus the host mean of the predictions, minus the target minus the host mean of the targets, and the metric is the mean absolute residual over such sites. A model that is constant within a host scores the spread of the targets around their host means, the zero-skill value. The host-mean MAE weights each host once and measures how well hosts are placed; it is descriptive. The host-mean oracle, which predicts every site with the true mean of its host, reaches a site-level MAE of \numGOnePrimaryDescriptiveCurvesMaeHostMeanOracleSixHundredFiftyFourMean{}~eV and marks what any predictor blind to the sites within a host can reach.

\textit{Advantage of the descriptors.} For each resample $r$ and budget $B$, $A_{rB} = \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{S} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{D}$ is computed from the seed-ensemble predictions on the same test sites; positive $A$ means that the DFT electronic descriptors lower the error. Negative values are reported as computed.

\textit{Hierarchical bootstrap.} Uncertainty comes from a paired hierarchical bootstrap \citep{Efron1979bootstrap,Efron1993introduction} with \numCfgAnalysisBootstrapDraws{} replicates: in each replicate the resamples are drawn with replacement, then the test hosts are drawn with replacement within each drawn resample with all their sites, every metric is recomputed, and the replicate value is the mean over the drawn resamples. One set of draws (generator seed \numGOnePrimaryBootstrapSeed{}) is shared by every model, budget and metric, so all contrasts are paired. Two-sided intervals are percentile intervals at level \numCfgAnalysisDisplayCi{}. Analyses on a single partition (the published split, the cross-validation and the leave-one-chemistry-out folds) use a paired host-cluster bootstrap instead.

\textit{Non-inferiority and $N^{*}$.} The margin is $\delta = \numCfgStatsDeltaEV{}$~eV. It is about one-sixth of the published structure-only error for neutral oxygen vacancies, \numCZeroOfficialComparisonEVKiyoharaPublished{}~eV \citep{Kiyohara2025machinelearning}, and well below the typical spread between DFT and experiment for defect formation energies \citep{Freysoldt2014firstprinciples}, so a difference smaller than $\delta$ would not change a physical conclusion about which oxides form vacancies easily. $N^{*}$ is the smallest tested budget at which the one-sided upper bound on $A$, the \numCfgAnalysisNStarUpperQuantile{} quantile of the bootstrap distribution, lies below $\delta$ and stays below $\delta$ at every larger tested budget. This is a non-inferiority test of S against D, one arm of a two-one-sided-tests procedure at level \numCfgAnalysisNStarAlpha{} \citep{Schuirmann1987comparison,Lakens2017equivalence}. If the first qualifying budget is the largest, $N^{*}$ is reported as reached only there, without extrapolation; if none qualifies, $N^{*}$ is reported as a lower bound. The ceiling on what pristine-host electronic information can add is $A$ at $B = \numCfgAnalysisCTwoCeilingBudget{}$ with its two-sided interval. The same rule, with the same $\delta$, is applied to the within-host residual MAE. The requirement that the bound hold at $N^{*}$ and at every larger budget is the stated protection against multiplicity; no further correction is applied.

\textit{Staging.} At each budget the contrasts $\mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{D} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{P}$, the cost of predicting the descriptors, and $\mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{P} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{S}$, staging against end-to-end learning, are estimated with the same bootstrap. $\mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{P} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{S}$ is classified, in this order, as P better or P worse when its two-sided interval at level \numCfgAnalysisStagingNullCi{} lies entirely below or above zero, as equivalent when its two-sided interval at level \numCfgAnalysisEquivalenceTostCi{} lies inside $\pm\numCfgAnalysisEquivalenceMarginEV{}$~eV, and as inconclusive otherwise. This equivalence rule replaced a null rule of the registered plan before any staged model was evaluated.

Both backbones are analysed with the same estimators and rules. Results of v2 are primary; results of v1 are reported alongside as the originally registered analysis.

\subsection{Geometry Robustness under a Machine-Learned Interatomic Potential}
\label{sec:mlip}

A model deployed on a new oxide will usually receive a structure relaxed with a universal interatomic potential rather than with DFT. To measure what that costs, each pristine host unit cell was relaxed with the medium MACE-MP-0 model \citep{Batatia2025foundation} (mace-torch \vermace{}) in double precision, cell and positions together, with the FIRE optimiser \citep{Bitzek2006structural} under the Fr\'echet cell filter of ASE \citep{HjorthLarsen2017atomic}, to a maximum force of \numCfgMlipRelaxFmaxEVPerA{}~\evperang{} in at most \numCfgMlipRelaxMaxSteps{} steps, without symmetry constraints. Hosts that do not converge are kept and flagged, and a sensitivity analysis excludes them.

The relaxed unit cell is tiled with the integer supercell matrix of the release supercell, and its atoms are mapped onto the release order by element and minimum-image position, so that the vacancy index of the release keeps its meaning. A host is first matched by a lattice translation alone, with a maximum residual of \numTilingVOneTolerancesResidualA{}~\AA{}. A host that fails is matched after exactly one lattice point operation of the unit cell, inversion or a two-fold rotation that preserves the cell metric, applied to the stored and the relaxed unit cells alike. These operations are isometries, so the relaxed energy and the strain are unchanged and only the atom correspondence uses them. With this rule, an extension of the registered protocol, all \numTilingVOneNHosts{} hosts are mapped.

The geometry change of each host is decomposed into three physical components. The isotropic strain is the cube root of the ratio of the relaxed to the DFT cell volume, minus one. The deviatoric strain is the Frobenius norm of the traceless part of the stretch tensor of the deformation between the DFT and relaxed lattices, after rigid rotation is removed by polar decomposition. The internal displacement is the root-mean-square displacement between the DFT positions and the relaxed fractional coordinates placed on the DFT lattice, after removal of the mean displacement.

Models trained on DFT geometry are evaluated, without retraining, under three conditions: the DFT geometry; the relaxed geometry; and, as a control, the relaxed geometry rescaled isotropically to the DFT volume. The change in MAE relative to the DFT geometry is computed for S, D and P at $B = \numCfgBudgetsMax{}$ and at $N^{*}$, or at \numCfgMlipBudgetIfNoNStar{} hosts if no $N^{*}$ exists. Under the relaxed geometry D keeps its DFT descriptors; it is then a fixed oracle, and its degradation isolates the contribution of the graph input alone. The per-host absolute change in error is regressed on the three geometry components and stratified by the $d$-shell occupancy of the cations (empty, filled, or other), with oxidation states from pymatgen \citep{Ong2013python}. The difference in degradation between P and S is classified with the equivalence rule of Section~\ref{sec:statistics}. If the median internal displacement is below \numCfgMlipNullRuleMedianInternalRmsdABelow{}~\AA{} and the intervals on the change in MAE include zero, the result is reported as a null. For v1 this is the registered analysis, on the relaxations of all hosts described above. For v2, the evaluation at $B = \numCfgBudgetsMax{}$ and at \numCfgMlipBudgetIfNoNStar{} hosts was run earlier on a separate set of relaxations of \numMlipInputsNMapped{} hosts, mapped by translation at a tolerance of \numMlipInputsMatchTolA{}~\AA{}; it is post hoc and descriptive.

\subsection{Latent Probe}
\label{sec:methods-probe}

To ask whether the structure-only model learns the electronic structure that D is given, the readout input vector of S (vacancy-node embedding, pooled embedding and global state) is extracted for every site from the first-seed checkpoint at every resample and budget. A ridge regression \citep{Pedregosa2018scikitlearn} maps these embeddings to each descriptor of D, with its regularisation strength chosen by a grouped \numCfgProbeAlphaCvFolds{}-fold cross-validation on the training sites, and the test coefficient of determination is reported per descriptor and as its mean over each descriptor class \citep{Alain2018understanding}. Two controls are run: the same probe on shuffled descriptor labels, and a randomly initialised, untrained encoder of the same architecture and hyperparameters. Linear recovery is said to hold if the class-mean coefficient of determination increases with budget (a Spearman correlation above zero with a bootstrap interval excluding zero) and exceeds both controls at $B \geq \numCfgProbePassRuleBeatsControlsMinBudget{}$. The probe of the primary backbone uses the S-v2 checkpoints and is post hoc; the registered probe uses the S-v1 checkpoints.

\subsection{Provenance Labels and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every analysis carries one of three labels, assigned by chronology rather than by outcome. \textit{Registered v1} marks an analysis of the originally registered backbone whose deciding rule was committed before its results existed. \textit{Post hoc} marks the v2 backbone and every rule committed after the results it decides. \textit{Extension} marks compute or analysis beyond the registered plan, such as the cross-validation, the precision extension and the extended tiling rule. The label of every result group is tabulated in the repository (\texttt{docs/provenance\_labels.md}), and every departure from the registered plan is logged with its date and reason before the affected run (\texttt{docs/deviations.md}); all are reported in the Supplementary Materials.

The code, the analysis plan, the run specifications and the splits are versioned in one repository. The release data, the graph store and the splits are fixed by checksum manifests, and model checkpoints by their hashes. Every run is identified by a hash of its full specification and its code reference; the cluster runs use the annotated tag \texttt{nrp-code-v1} as that reference, and a worker refuses a run unless the tag is an ancestor of its checkout and the source, scripts, configuration, splits and specifications are unchanged since the tag. Every result record stores the commit, the package versions and the seeds. The software used is matgl \vermatgl{}, PyTorch \vertorch{}, PyTorch Geometric \verpyg{}, mace-torch \vermace{}, scikit-learn \versklearn{}, Optuna \veroptuna{}, pymatgen \verpymatgen{} and ASE \verase{}. The repository, the result records and the predictions will be archived with a DOI on acceptance (Data Availability Statement).
