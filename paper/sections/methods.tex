\section{Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Every model predicts one physical quantity, the formation energy of the neutral oxygen vacancy, and every comparison is made on the same held-out oxides.

\subsection{Representation and Models}
\label{sec:models}

Each example is the pristine host supercell of the reference calculation (\numGraphsVOneStatsAtomsMin{} to \numGraphsVOneStatsAtomsMax{} atoms, median \numGraphsVOneStatsAtomsMedian{}), the cell in which the vacancy energy was computed, so the periodic images of the vacancy are separated exactly as in the reference energies. The oxygen to be removed stays in the structure and carries a vacancy flag that marks it (Figure~\ref{fig:models}b). Atoms closer than \numCfgGraphCutoffA{}~\AA{} are connected, so the graph is the neighbour list of the periodic crystal; each atom starts as a learned vector for its element, each bond as a Gaussian expansion of its length \citep{Chen2019graph}, and the host as a whole as a global state vector. The relaxed defect cell is not used, because the relaxation of the cations around the vacancy is an output of the very calculation that the model replaces; in wurtzite ZnO it moves no atom by more than \numStructureAuditZnoPristineVsRelaxedDisplacementAMaxOfMatchedAtoms{}~\AA{}.

The network is built from MEGNet blocks \citep{Chen2019graph} of matgl \vermatgl{} \citep{Ko2025materials} in PyTorch Geometric \citep{Fey2019fast,Paszke2019pytorch}. Each block passes information between bonded atoms, so successive blocks encode the environment of an atom over successive coordination shells: the first reaches the cations bonded to the flagged oxygen, the second their anion shells. The readout, the final step that maps the learned vectors to one energy, joins three of them: the vector of the vacancy site, which carries its local environment, and a summary over all atoms and the global state, which carry the thermodynamics of the host. A small fully connected network maps the joined vector to $E_\mathrm{f}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:network}). The split mirrors the variance of the target, which lies mostly between hosts (Section~\ref{sec:host_site}).

The structure-only network S reads the structure alone. The descriptor network D receives in addition the \numRfElectronicNFeatures{} pristine-cell electronic descriptors, \numNetDescriptorsHost{} host-level and \numNetDescriptorsSite{} site-level (Section~\ref{sec:data}), standardised with training-set statistics: the host-electronic class enters the initial global state and the site-electronic class the initial features of the vacancy node, so that each enters where its physics acts (a route chosen over injection at the readout by validation error, Appendix~\ref{app:network}). The descriptor predictor P1 has the architecture of S with one output per descriptor. The staged model P passes the descriptors predicted by P1 to the trained D of the same training set and seed, as in serial pipelines that predict a charge descriptor and then the energy \citep{Linton2026framework}, so the difference between P and D is the cost of predicting rather than computing the electronic structure.

Two kinds of baseline set the reference levels. The physics floor B0 is linear in \numCfgModelsPhysicsFloorNParameters{} host quantities, the oxide formation energy and the band gap, the thermodynamic and electronic controls of vacancy formation identified by Deml et al.\ \citep{Deml2014oxide,Deml2015intrinsic}; it is constant within a host. Random forests \citep{Breiman2001random,Pedregosa2018scikitlearn} of \numRfKumagaiHyperparametersNEstimators{} trees use all \numRfKumagaiNFeatures{} descriptors with the settings released by Kumagai et al.\ (RF-Kumagai) \citep{Kumagai2021insights}, the electronic descriptors only (RF-electronic) or the structural and compositional descriptors only (RF-structural). Every baseline is fitted on the same training hosts as the network.

\subsection{Training and Tuning}
\label{sec:training}

Training minimises the mean absolute error of the standardised $E_\mathrm{f}$ with AdamW \citep{Loshchilov2019decoupled}; an epoch is one pass of the optimiser over the training hosts, and the step size is reduced by a factor of \numCfgTrainingSchedulerFactor{} when the validation error has not improved for \numCfgTrainingSchedulerPatience{} epochs. A validation set, a fraction \numCfgTrainingValidationFrac{} of the training hosts and at least \numCfgTrainingValidationMinHosts{} of them, is drawn from every training set. Training stops when the validation error has not improved for \numCfgTrainingEarlyStoppingPatience{} epochs, or at \numCfgTrainingMaxEpochs{} epochs (Appendix~\ref{app:sens}), and keeps the weights of the best epoch. The number of training hosts counts the validation hosts, so it is the number of oxides with DFT labels that a model consumes. Every training set is trained from three random starting points, and the prediction at a site is the mean of the three networks, the seed ensemble; single networks scatter in $A$ by \numNetSpreadSixHundredFiftyFourSingleRunASd{}~eV at \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts, as much as the tolerance, so only ensembles are compared. GPU training is not bitwise reproducible, so every result is reported over resampled training sets, training seeds and a seed ensemble.

Hyperparameters, the settings of the network and optimiser that are not learned from the data, were chosen by the tree-structured Parzen estimator of Optuna \citep{Akiba2019optuna} with \numCfgTuningOptunaTrialsPerModelPerAnchor{} trials per model on each of three nested training sets of \numCfgTuningAnchorsItemOne{}, \numCfgTuningAnchorsItemTwo{} and \numCfgTuningAnchorsItemThree{} hosts. Under the default initialisation every model, S, D and P1, received the same search space and the same number of trials, and the objective was the validation error alone (for P1 the error over the standardised descriptors), with the test hosts removed from memory. Each training size takes the values of one of these sets (Appendix~\ref{app:network}).

With the default weight initialisation of the library, the network returned nearly identical energies for all oxygen sites of a host at small training sizes, which we found by inspecting held-out predictions: at \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{} training hosts the median spread of its predictions across the sites of a test host was \numSSiteResolutionForensicCheckpointsSTwoHundredFlagSweepTrainedMedianSdLabelled{}~eV, against \numSSiteResolutionForensicCheckpointsSTwoHundredFlagSweepTrainedMedianTargetSd{}~eV in the DFT energies. We changed the initialisation to He-uniform \citep{He2015delving} and re-tuned S and D on validation oxides only, with the same search space and \numCfgTuningOptunaTrialsPerModelPerAnchor{} trials on each of the three sets; test oxides played no part in that choice. P1 was not re-tuned: it was trained with the He initialisation and the settings tuned for it under the default initialisation, so the staged model P received no tuning trials under the initialisation used for S and D. The network resolves individual sites from about \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} training oxides and not below: the within-host spread of its predictions is \numNetSpreadRatioSTwentyFive{} and \numNetSpreadRatioSFifty{} of the DFT spread at \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemOne{} and \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemTwo{} oxides and \numNetSpreadRatioSOneHundred{} and \numNetSpreadRatioSTwoHundred{} at \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} and \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{}, against at most \numAblSpreadRatioSFifty{} with the default initialisation at every size up to \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{}. Appendix~\ref{app:ablation} compares the two initialisations.

\subsection{Splits}
\label{sec:splits}

Every split keeps all sites of one formula on the same side, so no compound is both trained on and tested (Appendix~\ref{app:splits}). In the learning-curve design each of \numCfgSplitNOuterResamples{} resamples holds out \numSplitMetaNTestHosts{} test hosts, stratified by the host-mean $E_\mathrm{f}$, and nests training sets of \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemOne{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemTwo{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{}, \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFive{} and \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemSix{} hosts in the remaining pool of \numSplitMetaPoolSize{}, so the test oxides of a resample are the same at every size while the training oxides change between resamples. At the two largest sizes, \numPrecisionABFourHundredBSixHundredFiftyFourSubsetsThirtyXFiveNResamples{} resamples with \numPrecisionABFourHundredBSixHundredFiftyFourSubsetsThirtyXFiveNSeeds{} seeds each, which contain the learning-curve runs, narrow the spread between resamples. Grouped $K$-fold cross-validation with $K = \numSplitCvKsItemOne{}$ and $K = \numSplitCvKsItemTwo{}$ over all \numSplitCvNHosts{} hosts tests every host once and trains on about \numNetCvTenHosts{} and \numNetCvTwentyHosts{} hosts per fold. Leave-one-family-out folds ($k = \numCfgRobustnessLocoK{}$) hold out whole families of cation chemistry, \numSplitLocoNFamilies{} families defined by the periodic-table groups of the cations. The published split of Kiyohara et al.\ \citep{Kiyohara2025machinelearning} enters only Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:statistics}

The metric is the site-level mean absolute error (MAE, eV) on held-out oxides, a mean over sites and not a bound for any single oxide. Because most of the variance of $E_\mathrm{f}$ lies between hosts (Section~\ref{sec:host_site}), a second metric isolates site resolution: the within-host residual MAE subtracts from each prediction and each target their means over the sites of the host, for test hosts with at least two sites. A predictor constant within a host scores the spread of the targets about their host means, the zero-skill value, and the host-mean oracle predicts every site with the true mean of its host. The descriptor advantage $A = \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{S} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{D}$ is computed from the seed-ensemble predictions on the same test sites, so positive $A$ means that the electronic descriptors lower the error; $A_\mathrm{within}$ is the same contrast on the within-host residual MAE.

Intervals come from a paired hierarchical bootstrap \citep{Efron1979bootstrap,Efron1993introduction} with \numCfgAnalysisBootstrapDraws{} replicates, which draws resamples and then test hosts with all their sites; one set of draws is shared by every model and training size, so every contrast is paired, and single partitions (cross-validation and family folds) use a paired host-cluster bootstrap (Appendix~\ref{app:stats}). Intervals are two-sided at \pctci{}, and $U$ is the one-sided upper bound of $A$, the \numCfgAnalysisNStarUpperQuantile{} quantile of its bootstrap distribution.

The tolerance $\delta = \numNetTolerance{}$~eV is set by the chemistry. It is about one-sixth of the published structure-only error of \numNetPublishedMae{}~eV \citep{Kiyohara2025machinelearning}, and an error of $\delta$ in $E_\mathrm{f}$ changes the equilibrium vacancy concentration, which scales as $\exp(-E_\mathrm{f}/k_\mathrm{B}T)$, by a factor of \numNetIllusTolFactor{} at \numNetIllusTemperature{}~K. The non-inferiority test asks whether the electronic descriptors still lower the site error by more than $\delta$ \citep{Schuirmann1987comparison,Lakens2017equivalence}: S is non-inferior to D at a training size where $U < \delta$, and $N^{*}$ is the smallest size from which $U < \delta$ holds at every larger size of the learning curve. The two networks match to within $\delta$ where, in addition, the two-sided interval of $A$ lies inside $\pm\delta$.

Staging is judged by $\mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{P} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{S}$, classified as P better or P worse when its two-sided interval lies below or above zero, as equivalent when its two-sided interval at level \numCfgAnalysisEquivalenceTostCi{} lies inside $\pm\delta_\mathrm{P}$ with $\delta_\mathrm{P} = \numNetStagingDeltaP{}$~eV, and as inconclusive otherwise (Appendix~\ref{app:stats}).

\subsection{Geometry and Probe Checks}
\label{sec:checks}

A network screening new oxides will usually receive structures relaxed with a machine-learned interatomic potential rather than with DFT. The pristine unit cells of \numNetGeomRelaxedHosts{} hosts were relaxed with MACE-MP-0 \citep{Batatia2025foundation}, cell and positions together, in ASE \citep{HjorthLarsen2017atomic}, tiled to the supercell of the release (Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}), and the trained models were evaluated without retraining on the relaxed cells and on the relaxed cells rescaled to the DFT volume. D keeps its DFT descriptors there, so its change isolates the structural input. A ridge regression from the readout input of S to each descriptor of D, set against shuffled labels and an untrained encoder, measures how much of the electronic structure the structure-only network exposes linearly \citep{Alain2018understanding} (Appendix~\ref{app:probe}).
