\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Structure Suffices}
\label{sec:disc_principal}

From \numNetNStar{} training oxides on, the electronic structure of the pristine host lowers the site error by at most \numNetHeadlineAUpper{}~eV, inside the tolerance, while with \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFive{} its advantage is not yet bounded below $\delta$ (Section~\ref{sec:learning_curves}). The descriptor forest RF-Kumagai is as accurate as either network or better up to \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} training oxides ($\mathrm{D} - \mathrm{RF} = \numNetCrossDMinusRfOneHundred{}$ [\numNetCrossDMinusRfOneHundredLo{}, \numNetCrossDMinusRfOneHundredHi{}]~eV there, \texttt{results/derived\_w2/w2\_forest\_crossover.json}); D lies below it with a \pctci{} interval from \numNetCrossDBudget{} oxides on and S from \numNetCrossSBudget{}. % claim NET-XRF
The question is why explicit electronic information stops paying for itself at a few hundred oxides.

Part of the answer is a fact about the descriptors. The band gap, the oxide formation energy, the dielectric response, the orbital character of the band edges, the Bader and Born effective charges and the centre of the oxygen $p$ band are all outputs of one DFT calculation on the pristine cell (Section~\ref{sec:data}), so each is fixed by the composition and atomic positions that the graph also receives. A map from the pristine structure to $E_\mathrm{f}$ can therefore absorb them, and the learning curve measures the sample complexity of doing so: how many oxides an encoder of the coordination environment needs before it reproduces the part of this information that matters for the vacancy. Descriptor studies tie the vacancy energy to the oxide formation enthalpy and the band gap \citep{Deml2014oxide,Deml2015intrinsic,Wexler2021factors}, and both are properties of the pristine lattice that the structure fixes.

We propose that what the descriptors still contribute is mainly host thermodynamics, which a structure-only network learns readily. Three measurements motivate the proposal. The host carries most of the variance of $E_\mathrm{f}$ ($\mathrm{ICC}_\mathrm{host} = \numVarianceDecompositionHostIcc{}$), and within a host the descriptors give D no advantage ($A_\mathrm{within} = \numNetWithinA{}$~eV, Section~\ref{sec:within_host}), so what they add acts between hosts. The descriptor predictor, which has the architecture of S, recovers the oxide formation energy best of all descriptors, with a coefficient of determination of \numStagingVTwoPOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourPerDescriptorDescFormationEnergyRTwoMean{}, and the host-electronic class better than the site-electronic class (\numStagingVTwoPOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourByClassHostElectronicClassMeanRTwo{} against \numStagingVTwoPOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourByClassSiteElectronicClassMeanRTwo{}, Section~\ref{sec:staging}). And the forest on structural and compositional descriptors alone, at \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfStructuralMaeMean{}~eV, is more accurate than the forest on electronic descriptors alone, at \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfElectronicMaeMean{}~eV, so what places a host can be read without an electronic-structure calculation; a linear probe recovers the descriptors from an untrained encoder of the same coordination environments as well as from the trained S (mean coefficient of determination \numProbeDescriptiveSVTwoAllCurvesInitZeroSixHundredFiftyFourMean{} against \numNetProbeAllRsqSixHundredFiftyFour{} with \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts, Appendix~\ref{app:probe}). The hypothesis makes two predictions. Given to D separately, the host-electronic class should carry nearly all of $A$ at every training size. For charged vacancies and shallow donors, whose energies depend on the position of the band edges directly rather than through the local bonding, explicit electronic information should keep its advantage to far larger training sets.

At the tuning and training length used, staging costs accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:staging}). We propose that the cost sits in the site-level descriptors, which describe the local ionic environment of the removed oxygen and which D reads at the vacancy node: P1 recovers the site-electronic class worse than the host-electronic class and the Bader charge of the oxygen to be removed at a coefficient of determination of only \numStagingVTwoPOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourPerDescriptorDescBaderChargeRTwoMean{}. P1 also kept the settings tuned under the default initialisation and reaches the training-length limit in \numNetCapPOneSixHundredFiftyFour{} of \numNetCapRunsSixHundredFiftyFour{} runs with \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts (Sections~\ref{sec:training} and~\ref{sec:staging}), so the measured cost includes whatever a re-tuned, longer-trained P1 would recover. Two checks separate the two sources: feeding D the true descriptors with noise added at the per-descriptor error of P1, class by class, and retraining P1 with its own tuning under the He initialisation and a longer limit.

\subsection{Local Vacancy Chemistry}
\label{sec:disc_site}

Within one oxide the vacancy energy changes from site to site far less than between oxides, and neither network captures most of that variation: with \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts the within-host residual MAE of S, \numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourSWithinHostMaeMean{}~eV, removes about a fifth of the zero-skill value of \numHostMeanOracleAggregateWithinHostMaeMean{}~eV (Section~\ref{sec:within_host}). We propose that within a host the vacancy energy is ordered by the local bonding and by the relaxation of the neighbouring cations rather than by the charge of the oxygen site. Two measurements motivate the proposal: the Bader charge ranks the sites of a host with a mean correlation of only \numWithinHostBreakdownSpearmanReferenceBaderChargeMean{}, and the pristine-site descriptors give D no within-host advantage ($A_\mathrm{within} = \numNetWithinA{}$ [\numNetWithinALo{}, \numNetWithinAHi{}]~eV). A breakdown of the within-host residuals by coordination number and M--O bond length would test the bonding part, and a comparison with the cation displacements in the relaxed defect cells of the release the relaxation part.

The spread is larger with filled cation $d$ shells: the zero-skill value is \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsDClassDZeroVTwoSZeroSkillMaePoint{}~eV in \dzero{} hosts and \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsDClassDTenVTwoSZeroSkillMaePoint{}~eV in hosts with a filled $d$ shell (Section~\ref{sec:within_host}). We propose that the bonding sets this difference. In closed-shell \dzero{} hosts the two electrons stay at the vacancy and sites differ mostly in the number of cation--oxygen bonds broken, which the neighbour list of the pristine structure encodes directly; with filled-$d$-shell cations such as Zn, Ga, Cd and Sn the M--O bonds are more covalent, and the cost depends also on which soft, polarisable cations are bonded to the site and on how the vacancy electrons hybridise with their $s$ states. The proposal predicts that the coordination number orders the sites of \dzero{} hosts better than those of filled-$d$-shell hosts, which the breakdown above would show. Two screened pairs bear on it. In Ga$_2$O$_3$, a filled-$d$-shell host, the four-coordinate site, with the most bonds to break, is the highest in DFT and in the prediction. In ZrO$_2$, a \dzero{} host, the four-coordinate site, with the longer mean Zr--O distance, lies below a three-coordinate site in DFT, and the network inverts the two (Section~\ref{sec:screening}): there the longer, weaker bonds outweigh their number, against the bond-count picture for \dzero{} hosts, and the network has not learned the distinction from \numNetScreenBudget{} oxides.

\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}
\label{sec:disc_prior}

Kumagai et al.\ built the database and showed that random forests on descriptors of the pristine host and site predict the neutral vacancy energy, with a learning curve over the number of training oxides \citep{Kumagai2021insights}; our RF-Kumagai reproduces their error to within \numBaselinesSummaryRfKumagaiVsPublishedDifferenceEV{}~eV. Kiyohara et al.\ showed that a graph network on the crystal structure alone reaches \numNetPublishedMae{}~eV for the neutral vacancy \citep{Kiyohara2025machinelearning}; the structure-only accuracy is theirs, and our network matches it on formula-grouped splits. Witman et al.\ predicted site-resolved vacancy formation enthalpies of oxides from the ideal crystal structure with defect graph networks \citep{Witman2023defect}, and Deml et al.\ identified the oxide formation enthalpy and the band gap as the controls of the vacancy energy \citep{Deml2014oxide,Deml2015intrinsic}, which B0 encodes. Linton, Singh and Aidhy predicted Bader charges and then vacancy energies in face-centred-cubic high-entropy alloys \citep{Linton2026framework}; our staged model applies that serial design to oxides with every electronic descriptor of the database.

What is new is the training size at which explicit pristine-host electronic information stops lowering the error of a structure-only network by more than a chemically chosen tolerance, measured for both inputs on the same held-out oxides with redrawn training sets and equal tuning, together with the site-level, staged and relaxed-geometry tests of the structure-only route.

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec:disc_scope}

The results describe deep, localised neutral vacancies in ordered non-magnetic oxides computed with one functional and one workflow (Section~\ref{sec:data}). The charged vacancies and the shallow-donor entries of the same database are the next test. The formation energy of $V_\mathrm{O}^{\bullet\bullet}$ depends on the electron chemical potential and so on the band-edge positions \citep{Freysoldt2014firstprinciples}, and the energy of a shallow donor is set by the conduction band into which its electrons delocalise \citep{Lany2005anion}; Section~\ref{sec:disc_principal} predicts that the descriptors keep their advantage there.

The domain leaves out many of the oxides in which vacancies are put to use. Among the screened candidates, In$_2$O$_3$, CeO$_2$ and WO$_3$ have no host in the release and SrTiO$_3$ and BaTiO$_3$ leave it as shallow donors (Section~\ref{sec:screening}), although they are among the most studied conducting, reducible and electrochromic oxides \citep{Ellmer2012past,Esch2005electron,Granqvist2000electrochromic}. Within the domain, the two energy groups of the screened sites follow the host band gap, with a rank correlation of \numScreeningDemoDescriptiveSpearmanDftVsBandGap{} across the \numNetScreenSites{} sites: we propose that the gap orders the groups because it marks where the two electrons go: in wide-gap, strongly bound oxides the broken bonds are costly and the electrons occupy a level deep in the gap, whereas the cation-$s$ conduction bands of ZnO, SnO$_2$ and Ga$_2$O$_3$ can take them up at lower cost. The rank correlation shows that the gap orders these sites; it does not establish the mechanism, because the gap also tracks bond strength and the sites come from a few named oxides. For cation families absent from training the advantage is not bounded below $\delta$ (Section~\ref{sec:learning_curves}).

No experimental formation energies were used to validate the predictions; every comparison is with the DFT energies of the release.
