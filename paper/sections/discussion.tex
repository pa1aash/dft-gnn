\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why structure can carry the electronic information of the pristine host}
\label{sec:disc_principal}

The principal result is a bound, not an equivalence. Once the networks had seen \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} host oxides, the DFT electronic structure of the pristine host and site, given explicitly, lowered the site error of $E_\mathrm{f}$ by less than $\delta = \numCfgStatsDeltaEV$~eV at the one-sided level on both networks, and the bound held at mean training sizes of \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTen{} and \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTwenty{} hosts in grouped cross-validation (Section~\ref{sec:learning_curves}). At \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFive{} hosts it did not hold: there the descriptors lowered the v1 error by \numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetFourHundredAMean{} [\numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetFourHundredACiLo{}, \numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetFourHundredACiHi{}]~eV. The descriptors also keep a small, measurable value on v2, where $A$ at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} hosts over thirty resamples (precision extension) is \numPrecisionABFourHundredBSixHundredFiftyFourVTwoThirtyXFiveSixHundredFiftyFourAMean{} [\numPrecisionABFourHundredBSixHundredFiftyFourVTwoThirtyXFiveSixHundredFiftyFourACiLo{}, \numPrecisionABFourHundredBSixHundredFiftyFourVTwoThirtyXFiveSixHundredFiftyFourACiHi{}]~eV, while on v1 the sign of $A$ turns negative beyond \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} hosts. What needs explaining is why explicit electronic information stops paying for itself at a few hundred oxides, not why it carries none.

One part of the explanation is a fact about the descriptors. The band gap, the oxide formation energy, the dielectric response, the band-edge orbital character, the Bader and Born effective charges and the centre of the oxygen $p$ band are all outputs of one DFT calculation on the pristine cell (Section~\ref{sec:descriptors}), so each is a function of the composition and atomic positions that the graph also receives. A map from the pristine structure to $E_\mathrm{f}$ can therefore in principle absorb them, and the learning curve measures sample complexity: how many oxides a learned encoder of the coordination environment needs before it reproduces the part of this information that matters for the vacancy. The coordination of the oxygen, the strength of its M--O bonds and the stability of the host lattice set how much energy the broken bonds cost and where the two electrons left behind can go, and all three are written in the structure.

We propose that the information the descriptors contribute at these data sizes is mainly host thermodynamics, which a structure-only network learns readily. Three measurements motivate this. First, the host accounts for most of the variance of $E_\mathrm{f}$ ($\mathrm{ICC}_\mathrm{host} = \numVarianceDecompositionHostIcc{}$), and the two-descriptor floor B0, linear in the oxide formation energy and the band gap, already reaches a site error of \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetBZeroMaeMean{}~eV. Second, the oxide formation energy is the descriptor that the descriptor predictor P1 recovers best from structure, with a coefficient of determination of \numStagingVOnePOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourPerDescriptorDescFormationEnergyRTwoMean{} on v1 and \numStagingVTwoPOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourPerDescriptorDescFormationEnergyRTwoMean{} on v2, and the host-electronic class is recovered better than the site-electronic class (Section~\ref{sec:staging}). Third, among the random-forest baselines, which are descriptive, the structural and compositional descriptors alone reach a site error of \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfStructuralMaeMean{} [\numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfStructuralMaeCiLo{}, \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfStructuralMaeCiHi{}]~eV at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} training hosts, against \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfElectronicMaeMean{} [\numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfElectronicMaeCiLo{}, \numBaselinesSummaryOuterFullBudgetRfElectronicMaeCiHi{}]~eV for the electronic descriptors alone, so what places a host can largely be read without an electronic-structure calculation. The probe does not decide the mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:disc_pipeline}). A test of the hypothesis would follow the learning curve of $A$ with the host-electronic and site-electronic classes given to D separately, and would extend the curve beyond \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTwenty{} hosts.

The sign difference between the networks may be a property of the networks rather than of the chemistry. At \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} hosts D has similar errors on both (\numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetSixHundredFiftyFourMAEDMean{} and \numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourDStateMaeMean{}~eV on v1 and v2), while the structure-only network is worse on v2 (\numCOneCTwoVTwoMaeSixHundredFiftyFourSMaeMean{} against \numGOnePrimaryPerBudgetSixHundredFiftyFourMAESMean{}~eV). We propose that the change of initialisation, adopted to address the site blindness of v1 at small budgets, made the structure-only network slightly less accurate at large ones, so that the positive $A$ of v2 measures in part a weaker S. A paired comparison of the two structure-only networks on identical hosts would test it.

\subsection{Site resolution: where the local vacancy chemistry is and is not captured}
\label{sec:disc_site}

Within one oxide the vacancy energy changes from site to site much less than between oxides, and no network captures most of this local chemistry. At \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} training hosts the within-host error of every model lies below the zero-skill value of \numHostMeanOracleAggregateWithinHostMaeMean{}~eV but removes only a modest part of it, and the pristine-site descriptors give D no within-host advantage over S on either network (Section~\ref{sec:within_host}). The Bader charge of the oxygen to be removed alone orders the sites of a host with a mean rank correlation of only \numWithinHostBreakdownSpearmanReferenceBaderChargeMean{} [\numWithinHostBreakdownSpearmanReferenceBaderChargeCiNinetyFiveItemOne{}, \numWithinHostBreakdownSpearmanReferenceBaderChargeCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}]. We propose that within a host the vacancy energy is ordered by the local bonding and the relaxation of the neighbouring cations rather than by the charge of the oxygen site, which would explain why site descriptors computed for the pristine cell add little within a host; a breakdown by coordination number and M--O bond length, fixed in advance as an exploratory analysis, would test it.

How S resolves sites depends on the network and its training, not only on the data. In the site-resolution diagnostics of v1, the difference between the outputs for inequivalent oxygen sites of one host was negligible at initialisation, came from the environment that the successive coordination shells give to the vacancy site, which the final step from learned vectors to energy reads (Supplementary Figure~S1), rather than from the vacancy flag itself, and was not created by training at budgets up to \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{} hosts (Section~\ref{sec:backbones}). Because the tuned settings change with the budget, the site resolution that S gains at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFive{} and \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} hosts cannot be attributed to the number of oxides alone. The site-resolution check also moved the vacancy flag only over the labelled sites of the test hosts, so resolution at the other oxygen sites of a supercell is untested.

The chemistry of the cations shapes the site-to-site spread (Supplementary Figure~S4). Hosts whose cations have an empty $d$ shell have a smaller spread than hosts with a filled $d$ shell (zero-skill \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsDClassDZeroVTwoSZeroSkillMaePoint{} against \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsDClassDTenVTwoSZeroSkillMaePoint{}~eV). We propose that in closed-shell \dzero{} hosts the two electrons stay localised at the vacancy and sites differ mostly in the number of cation--oxygen bonds broken, which the neighbour list of the pristine structure encodes, whereas in hosts of filled-$d$-shell cations (Zn, Ga, Cd, Sn) and lone-pair cations (Pb) the more covalent M--O bonds make the cost depend on the specific bonds to soft, polarisable cations and on how the vacancy electrons hybridise with the cation $s$ states. On v1, D is worse than S within Ga-containing hosts (S minus D \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationGaDifferencesSMinusDVOneWithinHostMaePoint{}~eV), consistent with the hypothesis that the pristine-site descriptors of such hosts vary between sites in ways that do not follow the vacancy energy. The site-level and within-host errors are both largest in the third of the hosts with the smallest band gaps (site error of S on v2 \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsBandGapTercileLowerVTwoSMaePoint{}~eV against \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsBandGapTercileUpperVTwoSMaePoint{}~eV in the third with the largest gaps); we propose that even the retained vacancies of these hosts hold their electrons in shallower, partly delocalised states, whose energy depends on the band edges and long-range screening more than on the local environment. In the screened oxides, the four-coordinate site has the highest DFT energy in Ga$_2$O$_3$ but lies below a three-coordinate site in ZrO$_2$, an order both networks invert (Table~\ref{tab:screening_pairs}); that longer, weaker bonds there outweigh the larger number of bonds is a hypothesis four pairs cannot test. Each reading rests on a breakdown with about a thousand uncorrected intervals, so an isolated group is weak evidence until replicated.

\subsection{The domain of the result}
\label{sec:disc_domain}

Every statement of this paper describes deep, localised neutral oxygen vacancies. The perturbed-host-state filter of the release removes the entries in which the two electrons of the neutral vacancy perturb the host band edge instead of occupying a localised state (Section~\ref{sec:domain}; Supplementary Figure~S2), and the removed entries are concentrated in hosts with a low-lying empty $d$ band: the flagged fraction runs from \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationMoPhsRateWith{} (Mo) to \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationNbPhsRateWith{} (Nb) for hosts containing Nb, W, Ti, V, Ta or Mo, against \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationZrPhsRateWith{} for Zr, also \dzero{} but with a higher-lying $d$ band. The models were trained on the neutral charge state only, on ordered hosts that are non-magnetic, and on formation energies computed with one functional, one set of corrections and one workflow \citep{Kumagai2021insights}. Excluding the \numSensHighMomentRfNExcludedEntries{} spin-polarised entries whose defect moment exceeds \numSensHighMomentVOneThresholdMuB{}~$\mu_\mathrm{B}$ left the bound on v1 below $\delta$ (Section~\ref{sec:sensitivities}), but every host was classified as non-magnetic in the release, and disordered or partially occupied hosts were never in the data (Section~\ref{sec:data}).

For charged vacancies and shallow donors we expect the conclusion to change, and this is a hypothesis. The formation energy of $V_\mathrm{O}^{\bullet\bullet}$ depends on the electron chemical potential and so on the position of the band edges \citep{Freysoldt2014firstprinciples}, and the energy of a shallow donor is set by the conduction band into which its electrons delocalise \citep{Lany2005anion}; both are host-electronic quantities that enter the energy directly rather than through the local bonding. Explicit electronic information may therefore keep its advantage to larger data sizes there, which the same comparison on the charged entries of the database would test.

The screening demonstration shows the domain in concrete terms. Of the named oxides of the analysis plan, ZnO, SnO$_2$, Ga$_2$O$_3$, TiO$_2$, ZrO$_2$, MgO and Al$_2$O$_3$ are in the universe, with \numScreeningDemoScopeNScreenedSites{} sites in \numScreeningDemoScopeNHosts{} hosts (Section~\ref{sec:screening}; an SnO$_2$ site is rendered in Supplementary Figure~S8). SrTiO$_3$ and BaTiO$_3$ are in the release but removed by the filter, monoclinic TiO$_2$ keeps one of its sites, and In$_2$O$_3$, CeO$_2$, WO$_3$, MoO$_3$, Nb$_2$O$_5$ and LaAlO$_3$ have no host in the release. Several of the absent oxides are among the most studied reducible, conducting and electrochromic oxides \citep{Ellmer2012past,Esch2005electron,Granqvist2000electrochromic}, so the models say nothing about many of the materials in which oxygen vacancies are exploited as donors or as sources of lattice oxygen. Within the domain, the DFT energies of the screened sites fall into a lower group (SnO$_2$, ZnO, Ga$_2$O$_3$, TiO$_2$) and a higher group (ZrO$_2$, MgO, Al$_2$O$_3$), and their rank correlation with the host band gap is \numScreeningDemoDescriptiveSpearmanDftVsBandGap{} across the ten sites, a descriptive value without an interval. We propose that in the wide-gap, strongly bound oxides the broken bonds are costly and the electrons left behind must occupy a level deep in a wide gap, whereas the cation-$s$ conduction bands of ZnO, SnO$_2$ and Ga$_2$O$_3$ accommodate them at lower cost; TiO$_2$, whose host formation energy per atom is more negative than that of MgO, falls in the lower group, which bond strength alone does not explain; it is also the screened oxide nearest the edge of the domain. With ten sites in seven hosts, the mean errors of \numScreeningDemoSummaryVTwoMaeEV{} (v2) and \numScreeningDemoSummaryVOneMaeEV{}~eV (v1) have wide, overlapping intervals and rank neither the networks nor the oxides.

\subsection{Staging, relaxed geometry and the probe as information about the pipeline}
\label{sec:disc_pipeline}

Staging costs accuracy because the descriptors it passes on are predicted with error. The identity $\mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{P} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{S} = -A - (\mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{D} - \mathrm{MAE}_\mathrm{P})$ means that a staged model can beat end-to-end learning from structure only where the descriptor advantage $A$ exceeds the cost of predicting the descriptors; at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} hosts $A$ is near zero on both networks, so even a perfect descriptor predictor could give P little gain there. P1 recovers the host-electronic class (\numStagingVOnePOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourByClassHostElectronicClassMeanRTwo{} on v1, \numStagingVTwoPOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourByClassHostElectronicClassMeanRTwo{} on v2) better than the site-electronic class (\numStagingVOnePOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourByClassSiteElectronicClassMeanRTwo{} and \numStagingVTwoPOneDescriptorsSixHundredFiftyFourByClassSiteElectronicClassMeanRTwo{}), and the Bader charge of the oxygen to be removed is among the worst recovered. We propose that the staging cost is set mainly by these site-level charges, which describe the local ionic environment of the removed oxygen and which the pristine structure determines in principle but a network trained on a few hundred oxides does not yet reproduce; the better recovery of the site class on v2 is consistent with its smaller staging cost. Feeding D the true descriptors with added noise at the per-descriptor error of P1, class by class, would test this.

The networks tolerate the structures that a universal interatomic potential provides. MACE-MP-0 expanded every host (median isotropic strain \numGeometryDescriptiveComponentsAllHostsEpsVMedian{}), and on v1 the error of S rose by \numGeometryCThreeBBudgetsSixHundredFiftyFourDeltaMaeSMlipMean{}~eV, far inside $\delta$, while the change of D, which keeps its DFT descriptors, had an interval including zero (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). Although the isotropic strain is the only component whose regression coefficient excludes zero, rescaling the relaxed cells to the DFT volume did not reduce the change of S. We propose, as a hypothesis and not a result, that S responds to the cell shape and internal coordinates that the rescaled cell keeps, the M--O bond lengths and polyhedral distortions, more than to the volume itself; a per-host comparison of the relaxed and rescaled changes would test it. No v2 network was evaluated on these relaxations.

The probe's failure to exceed an untrained encoder means that the data do not show that training on formation energies builds a linear encoding of the electronic descriptors (Section~\ref{sec:probe}; Supplementary Figure~S7). This neither supports nor excludes an implicit, nonlinear encoding. That an untrained encoder already exposes much of the descriptor variance is consistent with the descriptors being largely functions of composition and local geometry, the premise of Section~\ref{sec:disc_principal}.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:disc_prior}

Kumagai et al.\ built the database and showed that random forests on descriptors of the pristine host and site predict the neutral vacancy energy, with a learning curve over training oxides averaged over repeated draws \citep{Kumagai2021insights}; our RF-Kumagai reproduces their published error to within \numBaselinesSummaryRfKumagaiVsPublishedDifferenceEV{}~eV. Kiyohara et al.\ trained a graph network on the crystal structure alone and reported \numCZeroOfficialComparisonEVKiyoharaPublished{}~eV for the neutral vacancy on a single partition \citep{Kiyohara2025machinelearning}; on their split our S of v1 reaches \numCZeroOfficialSEnsembleMaePoint{}~eV, a reproduction and not an improvement, and the structure-only result is theirs. Witman et al.\ predicted site-resolved vacancy formation enthalpies of oxides from the ideal crystal structure with a defect graph network \citep{Witman2023defect}, and Deml et al.\ identified the oxide formation enthalpy and the band gap as controls of the vacancy energy \citep{Deml2014oxide,Deml2015intrinsic}, which our floor B0 encodes. Linton, Singh and Aidhy predicted Bader charges and then vacancy energies in face-centred-cubic high-entropy alloys \citep{Linton2026framework}; our staged model is a comparison on oxides, with a full structure graph and all the electronic descriptors of D, and not a replica of their serial design in a metal.

What is new here is what the Introduction states: the number of training hosts at which explicit pristine-host electronic information stops lowering the error of a structure-only network by more than a stated tolerance, and the residual improvement that perfect information on the pristine host still gives at the largest size, measured on grouped splits with redrawn training sets and equal tuning for every model. The site-resolution, staging, geometry and probe analyses are secondary; the structure-only accuracy, descriptor learning curves, the serial design and the data are not new.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc_limitations}

The results rest on one database computed with one functional and one workflow, on the neutral charge state only, and on ordered, non-magnetic hosts in which the vacancy is deep; nothing was tested outside that domain or at other levels of theory. The bound on $A$ is established only up to the largest data size available, a mean of \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTwenty{} training hosts in cross-validation, whose test population differs from that of the budget points (Supplementary Figure~S3); nothing is known beyond it, and under leave-one-chemistry-out folds the bound holds on v1 only ($U = \numIngestMOneRecomputeLocoVTwoAUpperOneSided{}$~eV on v2). The primary network was adopted after diagnostics of the originally registered network on test hosts, so every v2 result is post hoc; v1 is reported in full beside it, and the two disagree in the sign of $A$ at and beyond \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemSix{} hosts. The GPU model was not randomised against resample, fold or seed, and runs on different models are not bitwise reproducible, so hardware effects cannot be separated from sampling (Supplementary Figure~S5). The limit of \numCfgTrainingMaxEpochs{} passes over the training hosts stopped training of S more often than of D, and the reruns with a longer limit exist for v1 only (Supplementary Figure~S6). Neither a v2 geometry evaluation on our relaxations nor the v2 true-descriptor swap-in check of the staged model exists, and the second-architecture check is one untuned resample. The within-host breakdown and the other secondary analyses carry many intervals without a multiplicity correction, the tuning used one resample whose hosts overlap the test hosts of others, and the screening demonstration covers ten sites in seven hosts.

For practice, within the deep-vacancy domain and for neutral vacancies in oxides of chemical families represented in training, a practitioner with formation energies for \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} or more host oxides may skip the DFT calculation of the pristine host's electronic structure and lose less than $\delta$ in site error. With \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFive{} or fewer training oxides that calculation lowered the error by an amount the tolerance could not exclude, so it should be kept there; it should also be kept for cation chemistries absent from training, where the bound held on v1 only, and for oxides likely to host shallow donors and for charged vacancies, where nothing was measured.
