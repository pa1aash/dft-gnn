The runs that reach the limit lower $A$ slightly rather than raise it. With \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts, the single-run site MAE of the runs of S that reach the limit minus that of the other runs of S is \numNetCapThirtyByFiveCappedMinusUncappedMaeS{}~eV over the additional resamples and \numNetCapSweepCappedMinusUncappedMaeS{}~eV in the learning-curve design: they are the runs still improving when training stopped. Pairs of S and D runs that contain such a run have a smaller $A$, so including them changes the mean single-run $A$ by \numNetCapThirtyByFiveEffectOnA{}~eV over the additional resamples and by \numNetCapSweepEffectOnA{}~eV in the learning-curve design, and removing them from the seed ensembles moves $A$ over the additional resamples from \numNetCapEnsembleAAll{} to \numNetCapEnsembleACappedRemoved{}~eV. Training S longer would therefore more likely shrink $A$ than grow it; the effect is small next to the interval of $A$ and is descriptive. For P1 the limit binds in most runs at \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts, so the staging comparison of Section~\ref{sec:staging} holds for P1 trained within that limit.

\subsection{Entries with a High Defect Moment}

The hosts are classified as non-magnetic in the release, but \numSensHighMomentRfNExcludedEntries{} neutral vacancy calculations of the dataset end with a defect magnetic moment above \numSensHighMomentVOneThresholdMuB{}~$\mu_\mathrm{B}$; all lie in La- or Ce-containing oxides, apart from one tungstate. The check removes these entries from training and test and repeats the fit. For RF-Kumagai with \numSensHighMomentRfPoolSize{} training hosts the removal changes the site MAE by \numSensHighMomentRfDeltaTotalExcludedMinusBaselineMaeMean{} [\numSensHighMomentRfDeltaTotalExcludedMinusBaselineMaeCiLo{}, \numSensHighMomentRfDeltaTotalExcludedMinusBaselineMaeCiHi{}]~eV (\texttt{results/sens\_high\_moment\_rf.json}), because the entries make up a small part of every test set.

\subsection{A Second Architecture}

An untuned CGCNN \citep{Xie2018crystal}, with fixed optimiser settings, was trained as S and D on resample \numSensCgcnnResample{} at \numSensCgcnnBudgetsItemOne{}, \numSensCgcnnBudgetsItemTwo{} and \numSensCgcnnBudgetsItemThree{} training hosts (\texttt{results/sens\_cgcnn.json}). Its advantage falls with the number of training hosts as that of the network does, from \numSensCgcnnTableCgcnnFiftyAPoint{} [\numSensCgcnnTableCgcnnFiftyACiNinetyFiveItemOne{}, \numSensCgcnnTableCgcnnFiftyACiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}]~eV at \numSensCgcnnBudgetsItemOne{} hosts to \numSensCgcnnTableCgcnnSixHundredFiftyFourAPoint{} [\numSensCgcnnTableCgcnnSixHundredFiftyFourACiNinetyFiveItemOne{}, \numSensCgcnnTableCgcnnSixHundredFiftyFourACiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}]~eV at \numSensCgcnnBudgetsItemThree{}, against \numSensCgcnnTableMegnetVTwoFiftyAPoint{} and \numSensCgcnnTableMegnetVTwoSixHundredFiftyFourAPoint{} [\numSensCgcnnTableMegnetVTwoSixHundredFiftyFourACiNinetyFiveItemOne{}, \numSensCgcnnTableMegnetVTwoSixHundredFiftyFourACiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}]~eV for the network on the same resample. CGCNN has \numSensCgcnnParameterCountsCgcnnCgcnnSSixHundredFiftyFour{} trainable parameters in S, fewer than the network at any tuning size (\numSensCgcnnParameterCountsMegnetVTwoSTwoHundred{} to \numSensCgcnnParameterCountsMegnetVTwoSSixHundredFiftyFour{}). With one resample the intervals reflect only the sampling of its test hosts, so the check shows that the fall of $A$ with training data is not peculiar to MEGNet blocks; it sets no bound for other architectures.
