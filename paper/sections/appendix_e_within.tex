\section{Within-Host Breakdown}
\label{app:within}

This appendix asks whether the ability of the network to order the oxygen sites of one host depends on the chemistry of the host or on the site, with \numWithinHostBreakdownBudget{} training hosts. The analysis reads the existing held-out predictions and trains nothing (\texttt{results/within\_host\_breakdown.json}). The measure is the within-host residual MAE: for a host with at least two tested sites, the prediction and the DFT energy of each site are taken relative to their host means, and the mean absolute difference of these relative values is the error that remains once every host-level offset is removed. Its reference is the zero-skill value, the mean absolute deviation of the DFT energies from their host mean, which any predictor constant within a host reaches, such as B0 or a network blind to the site. A model has within-host skill in a group when its within-host residual MAE lies below the zero-skill value.

Each site's prediction is the seed ensemble averaged over the resamples in which its host was tested, so the breakdown covers \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsPooledAllNHosts{} hosts and \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsPooledAllNSites{} sites, \numNetWithinHosts{} hosts and \numNetWithinSites{} sites of them with at least two tested sites. Because this average is taken per site rather than per resample, its values are comparable with each other and not with the learning-curve values of Section~\ref{sec:within_host}. Intervals come from a host-cluster bootstrap within each group with \numWithinHostBreakdownBootstrapDraws{} draws, paired between S and D.

Over all hosts both models resolve sites within a host (Table~\ref{tab:app_within}): the within-host residual MAE is \numNetWithinMaeS{} [\numNetWithinMaeSLo{}, \numNetWithinMaeSHi{}]~eV for S and \numNetWithinMaeD{} [\numNetWithinMaeDLo{}, \numNetWithinMaeDHi{}]~eV for D, against a zero-skill value of \numNetWithinZeroSkill{} [\numNetWithinZeroSkillLo{}, \numNetWithinZeroSkillHi{}]~eV, and the descriptors leave it unchanged, S $-$ D = \numNetWithinSMinusD{} [\numNetWithinSMinusDLo{}, \numNetWithinSMinusDHi{}]~eV. The chemistry enters mainly through the spread of the target itself. Hosts whose cations have an empty $d$ shell have a smaller site-to-site spread than hosts with a filled $d$ shell, with zero-skill values of \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsDClassDZeroVTwoSZeroSkillMaePoint{} and \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsDClassDTenVTwoSZeroSkillMaePoint{}~eV, and both models keep within-host skill in both classes. Across the thirds of host stability the within-host residual MAE of S barely changes (\numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsStabilityTercileLowerVTwoSWithinHostMaePoint{} to \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsStabilityTercileUpperVTwoSWithinHostMaePoint{}~eV), while its site error rises from \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsStabilityTercileLowerVTwoSMaePoint{}~eV in the most stable third to \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsStabilityTercileUpperVTwoSMaePoint{}~eV in the least stable third, so the error of the least stable hosts lies in their host-level offset rather than in the ordering of their sites. The smallest-gap third has the largest within-host residual MAEs of both models and the largest site error of S, \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsBandGapTercileLowerVTwoSMaePoint{}~eV, and the errors at the most and least negative Bader charges of the vacancy oxygen within a host are close to each other (Figure~\ref{fig:app_within}).

The breakdown is descriptive and uncorrected for multiplicity. It reports an interval for every group, model and metric, so some intervals exclude zero by chance alone, and an isolated interval that excludes zero is weak evidence. Of the S $-$ D intervals of the per-cation groups, three exclude zero: Na (\numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationNaDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaePoint{} [\numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationNaDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaeCiNinetyFiveItemOne{}, \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationNaDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaeCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}]~eV) and Sn (\numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationSnDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaePoint{} [\numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationSnDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaeCiNinetyFiveItemOne{}, \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationSnDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaeCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}]~eV) in favour of D, and Cd (\numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationCdDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaePoint{} [\numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationCdDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaeCiNinetyFiveItemOne{}, \numWithinHostBreakdownGroupsCationCdDifferencesSMinusDVTwoWithinHostMaeCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}]~eV) in favour of S; none of the groups of Table~\ref{tab:app_within} excludes zero. Groups with fewer than ten hosts carry no interval. The cation groups overlap, because a host with several cations is counted under each, and they are confounded with each other and with structure type. Every group describes the dataset, that is, deep, localised neutral vacancies only.
