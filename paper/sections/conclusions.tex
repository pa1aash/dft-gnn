\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For deep, localised neutral oxygen vacancies in ordered oxides of the database of Kumagai et al.\ classified as non-magnetic in the release, a graph network that reads only the pristine host structure, with the vacancy site marked, came within $\delta = \numCfgStatsDeltaEV$~eV in site error of the same network given the DFT descriptors of the pristine host and site once trained on \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} host oxides. This one-sided bound held on the primary network, whose results are post hoc, and on the originally registered network, at \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} hosts and at mean training sizes of \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTen{} and \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTwenty{} hosts in grouped cross-validation, but not at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFive{}. It is a bound, not an equivalence: on the primary network the descriptors still lowered the error, with an interval excluding zero, and on the originally registered network the sign of their advantage reversed beyond \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} hosts. Within a host the pristine-site descriptors gave no advantage. Predicting the descriptors from structure never beat structure alone, geometry relaxed with a machine-learned interatomic potential changed the error of the originally registered structure-only network by far less than $\delta$, and a linear probe gave no evidence that training encodes the descriptors beyond what an untrained encoder exposes. Within this domain, with formation energies for \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} oxides available, the electronic-structure calculation of each new pristine host can be omitted at a cost below $\delta$ in mean absolute error over held-out oxides, not a bound for any single oxide; with fewer oxides, for shallow donors, charged vacancies or cation chemistries absent from training, the measurements do not support omitting it.
