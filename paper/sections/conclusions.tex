\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For the deep, localised neutral oxygen vacancy in ordered non-magnetic oxides, the pristine crystal structure with the vacancy site marked carries the information that the DFT electronic structure of the host adds to the formation energy, once a graph network has learned from several hundred computed oxides. From \numNetNStar{} training oxides the structure-only network matches the same network given every pristine-host descriptor to within \numCfgStatsDeltaEV{}~eV: the descriptors lower the site error by \numNetHeadlineA{} [\numNetHeadlineALo{}, \numNetHeadlineAHi{}]~eV, they place each oxide rather than order the sites within it, and predicting them from structure never beats learning the energy from structure directly.

For screening, the number of computed oxides at hand fixes the model. With \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} training oxides or fewer, a random forest on the pristine-host descriptors is as accurate as either network or better; between \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} and \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{} the descriptor network overtakes it and remains the most accurate model up to \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFive{}; from \numNetNStar{} training oxides on, the structure-only network matches it, needs no DFT calculation of a new host and tolerates host structures relaxed with a machine-learned interatomic potential.
