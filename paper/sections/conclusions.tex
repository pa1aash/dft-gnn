\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For the deep, localised neutral oxygen vacancy in ordered non-magnetic oxides, the pristine crystal structure with the vacancy site marked reproduces, to within the tolerance, the accuracy of the descriptor network, once a graph network has learned from several hundred computed oxides. From \numNetNStar{} training oxides the structure-only network matches the same network given the \numRfElectronicNFeatures{} pristine-cell electronic descriptors to within \numNetTolerance{}~eV: the descriptors still lower the site error, by $A = \numNetHeadlineA{}$ [\numNetHeadlineALo{}, \numNetHeadlineAHi{}]~eV with $U = \numNetHeadlineAUpper{}$~eV, their benefit lies between oxides, not within them, and, at the tuning and training length used, predicting them from structure never beats learning the energy from structure directly.

For screening, the number of computed oxides at hand fixes the model. With \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} training oxides or fewer, a random forest on the pristine-host descriptors is as accurate as either network or better; between \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} and \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFour{} the descriptor network overtakes it and remains the most accurate model up to \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFive{}; from \numNetNStar{} training oxides on, the structure-only network matches it, needs no DFT calculation of a new host and is insensitive, at the error measured, to host structures relaxed with a machine-learned interatomic potential.
