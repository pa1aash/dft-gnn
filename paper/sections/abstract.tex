The formation energy of the neutral oxygen vacancy sets how readily an oxide gives up lattice oxygen, and so its conductivity, reactivity and ionic transport. Machine-learned surrogates predict it from density-functional theory (DFT) descriptors of the pristine host or from the crystal structure alone. We asked at how many training hosts explicit pristine-host electronic information stops improving a structure-only graph network. Using \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalSites{} neutral vacancy sites in \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalHosts{} oxides from the database of Kumagai et al., we trained a graph network on the pristine supercell with the removed oxygen site marked, with and without DFT descriptors, on \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemOne{} to \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts grouped by formula, and tested whether the descriptors lowered the site error by more than a tolerance of \numCfgStatsDeltaEV{}~eV. On the primary network, adopted post hoc after diagnostics of the originally registered one, the descriptor advantage at \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} training hosts was \numCOneCTwoVTwoAMaeSixHundredFiftyFourA{}~eV (two-sided interval \numCOneCTwoVTwoAMaeSixHundredFiftyFourCiNinetyFiveItemOne{} to \numCOneCTwoVTwoAMaeSixHundredFiftyFourCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}~eV). On both networks its one-sided upper bound lay below the tolerance at \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{}, \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTen{} and \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTwenty{} training hosts (the last two cross-validation means), but not at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFive{}. The result applies to deep, localised neutral oxygen vacancies in ordered hosts classified as non-magnetic in the release. Within hosts the descriptors did not improve site ordering, predicting them from structure never beat structure alone, and geometry relaxed with a machine-learned interatomic potential changed the originally registered network's error far less than the tolerance.
