The formation energy of the neutral oxygen vacancy sets how readily an oxide gives up lattice oxygen, and with it its conductivity, reactivity and ionic transport. Machine-learned surrogates predict it from density-functional theory (DFT) descriptors of the pristine host or from the crystal structure alone. We asked at how many training oxides explicit pristine-host electronic information stops improving a structure-only graph network. Using \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalSites{} neutral vacancy sites in \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalHosts{} non-magnetic oxides from the database of Kumagai et al., we trained a graph network on the pristine supercell with the removed oxygen site marked, with and without the DFT descriptors, on \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemOne{} to \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training hosts split by compound, and tested whether the descriptors lowered the site error by more than a tolerance of \numCfgStatsDeltaEV{}~eV. On the primary network, adopted post hoc after diagnostics of the originally registered one, the advantage of the descriptors at \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{} training hosts was \numCOneCTwoVTwoAMaeSixHundredFiftyFourA{}~eV (two-sided interval \numCOneCTwoVTwoAMaeSixHundredFiftyFourCiNinetyFiveItemOne{} to \numCOneCTwoVTwoAMaeSixHundredFiftyFourCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}~eV). On both networks its one-sided upper bound lay below the tolerance at \numGOnePrimaryNStarNStar{}, \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTen{} and \numCvExtendedBudgetXTrainingHostsKTwenty{} training hosts (the last two cross-validation means), but not at \numGOnePrimaryNStarBudgetsTestedItemFive{}. The result applies to deep, neutral vacancies in ordered, non-magnetic hosts. Within hosts the descriptors did not improve site ordering, predicting them from structure never beat structure alone, and geometry relaxed with an interatomic potential changed the originally registered network's error far less than the tolerance.
