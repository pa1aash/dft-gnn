The formation energy of the neutral oxygen vacancy governs lattice-oxygen release, and with it conductivity, catalysis and ion transport. Surrogates predict it from density-functional theory (DFT) descriptors of the pristine host or from structure alone. We asked how many computed oxides a graph network needs before structure alone suffices. Using \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalSites{} vacancy sites in \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalHosts{} oxides from the Kumagai database, we trained one network on the pristine supercell with the vacancy site marked, with and without all pristine-host descriptors, on \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemOne{} to \numCfgBudgetsMax{} training oxides grouped by formula. From \numNetHeadlineHosts{} oxides the structure-only network matches the descriptor network to within \numCfgStatsDeltaEV{}~eV: the descriptors lower the error by \numNetHeadlineA{}~eV (\pctci{} interval \numNetHeadlineALo{}--\numNetHeadlineAHi{}~eV). At \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} oxides or fewer a descriptor-based forest is as accurate or better, and at \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFive{} the advantage is not yet bounded below \numCfgStatsDeltaEV{}~eV. The descriptors do not improve the ranking of sites within an oxide. Predicting them from structure never beats structure alone, and machine-learned relaxation of the host changes the structure-only error by less than \numMlipEvalVTwoDMAESSixHundredFiftyFourMlipCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}~eV. For deep neutral vacancies in ordered non-magnetic oxides, several hundred training oxides make the descriptor DFT step unnecessary, except across oxide families.
