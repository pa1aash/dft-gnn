The formation energy of the neutral oxygen vacancy governs lattice-oxygen release, and with it conductivity, catalysis and ion transport. Surrogates predict it from density-functional theory (DFT) descriptors or from structure alone. We asked how many computed oxides a graph network needs before structure suffices. Using \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalSites{} vacancy sites in \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalHosts{} oxides from the Kumagai database, we trained one network on the pristine supercell with the vacancy site marked, with and without \numRfElectronicNFeatures{} pristine-cell electronic descriptors (\numNetDescriptorsHost{} host-level, \numNetDescriptorsSite{} site-level), on \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemOne{} to \numCfgBudgetsMax{} formula-grouped training oxides. From \numNetHeadlineHosts{} oxides the structure-only network matches the descriptor network to within \numNetTolerance{}~eV: the descriptors lower the error by \numNetHeadlineA{}~eV (\pctci{} interval \numNetHeadlineALo{}--\numNetHeadlineAHi{}~eV). At \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemThree{} oxides or fewer a descriptor forest is as accurate or better, and at \numCfgBudgetsHostsItemFive{} the advantage is not yet bounded below \numNetTolerance{}~eV. The descriptors give no measurable improvement in the ranking of sites within an oxide. At the tuning and training length used, predicting them from structure never beats structure alone, and machine-learned host relaxation changes the structure-only error by less than \numMlipEvalVTwoDMAESSixHundredFiftyFourMlipCiNinetyFiveItemTwo{}~eV. For deep neutral vacancies in ordered non-magnetic oxides, several hundred training oxides make the descriptor DFT step dispensable at the \numNetTolerance{}~eV tolerance, except across oxide families.
