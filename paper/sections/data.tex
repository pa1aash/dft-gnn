\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsubsection{Source and target}

All formation energies, descriptors and structures come from the high-throughput database of neutral and charged oxygen vacancies of Kumagai et al.\ \citep{Kumagai2021insights}, released under the \ccby{} licence and pinned in this work to commit \releasecommit{} of the public release. No new density-functional calculation was performed. The target is the formation energy $E_\mathrm{f}$ of the neutral oxygen vacancy, $V_\mathrm{O}^{\times}$, at the reference oxygen chemical potential of the release. The host structures are the PBEsol(+$U$)-relaxed pristine crystals of the release, and each vacancy was relaxed in a supercell of its host \citep{Kumagai2021insights}. The neutral vacancy leaves two electrons behind when the oxygen atom is removed; where they settle, in a localised state on the cations around the vacancy or in the host band edge, is the electronic question that the descriptors of Section~\ref{sec:descriptors} encode.

The database groups by host as strongly as the defect chemistry suggests it should. Grouped by host, the one-way random-effects intraclass correlation of $E_\mathrm{f}$ \citep{Shrout1979intraclass} over the final universe is $\mathrm{ICC}_\mathrm{host} = \numVarianceDecompositionHostIcc{}$ (two-sided interval \numVarianceDecompositionHostIccCiLo{} to \numVarianceDecompositionHostIccCiHi{}): the between-host variance component is \numVarianceDecompositionHostSigmaTwoBetween{}~\eVsq{} and the within-host component \numVarianceDecompositionHostSigmaTwoWithin{}~\eVsq{}. The host fixes the thermodynamic stability of the lattice and the position of the band edges that receive the two electrons; the individual oxygen site adjusts this through its cation coordination and local bonding. This host-versus-site structure shapes every metric used in this work (Section~\ref{sec:statistics}).

\subsubsection{Filter cascade}

The release holds \numFilterCascadeStepsReleaseSites{} completed neutral calculations in \numFilterCascadeStepsReleaseHosts{} hosts (Table~\ref{tab:filter_cascade}; the cascade is drawn in the Supplementary Materials). Kumagai et al.\ assembled a neutral machine-learning subset of \numFilterCascadeStepsMlSubsetSites{} vacancies in \numFilterCascadeStepsMlSubsetHosts{} hosts, from which entries with perturbed host states, entries whose band edges could not be determined and dynamically unstable hosts had already been removed \citep{Kumagai2021insights}. Three further filters were fixed before any model was trained and applied in order. D1 removes the \numFilterCascadeStepsDOneNRemoved{} entries flagged as a split vacancy, an unknown defect type or a final configuration different from the initial one; in each of them the oxygen atom did not stay a simple vacancy, so the label no longer describes the structure given to the model. D2 removes the host Sr$_{2}$Zr$_{7}$O$_{16}$ (\numFilterCascadeStepsDTwoNRemoved{} entries), whose site labels in the machine-learning table do not match the release targets. D3 keeps an entry of the two hosts with inconsistent cell metadata only if its vacancy site maps to an oxygen atom with a matching first cation shell; it removes nothing. The final universe (universe v1) contains \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalSites{} neutral oxygen-vacancy sites in \numFilterCascadeStepsFinalHosts{} non-magnetic oxide hosts, each host a distinct formula. A host is a crystal structure and a site is one symmetry-inequivalent oxygen of it; the release has no polymorphs, so grouping by formula and grouping by host coincide.

\subsubsection{Descriptor classes}
\label{sec:descriptors}

Kumagai et al.\ provide \numDescriptorProvenanceNDescriptorColumns{} descriptors per site \citep{Kumagai2021insights}. We assigned each to one of three classes by the calculation needed to obtain it for a new oxide (repository file \texttt{docs/}\allowbreak\texttt{descriptor\_taxonomy.csv}). The two electronic classes together hold \numRfElectronicNFeatures{} descriptors. The host-electronic class holds properties of the pristine host from density-functional theory: the PBEsol(+$U$) band gap, the formation energy of the oxide per atom, the electronic and ionic dielectric constants, and the $s$, $p$, $d$ and $f$ orbital character of the valence-band maximum and the conduction-band minimum. The site-electronic class holds properties of the pristine oxygen site from the same calculation: its Bader charge and volume \citep{Henkelman2006fast}, its Born effective charge, the corresponding statistics over its cation neighbours, and the centre of its oxygen $p$ band relative to the valence-band maximum. The structural and compositional class (\numRfStructuralNFeatures{} descriptors) holds the electronegativities of the neighbouring cations and the elemental composition, which need no electronic-structure calculation.

The descriptor network D (Section~\ref{sec:models}) receives only the two electronic classes. The reason is physical. The structural and compositional class is, in principle, a function of the crystal structure that every graph network already reads, so giving it to D would not separate what structure alone can supply from what an electronic-structure calculation adds. The electronic classes carry the quantities that the defect chemistry of the neutral vacancy points to: the band gap and the orbital character of the conduction-band minimum set where the two electrons left by the vacancy can go and at what cost, the oxide formation energy measures how strongly oxygen is bound in the lattice, and the Bader charges and Born effective charges of the site describe how ionic its bonding is \citep{Kumagai2021insights,Deml2014oxide}. Every descriptor of both classes is computed from the pristine unit cell; none requires a defect calculation, so D is an oracle with respect to pristine electronic structure only, not with respect to the defect.

\subsubsection{Domain: deep neutral vacancies}
\label{sec:domain}

The perturbed-host-state filter decides which oxides the models can describe. In a perturbed-host-state entry the two electrons of the neutral vacancy do not occupy a localised in-gap state; they perturb the host band edge, so the vacancy behaves as a shallow donor whose electrons delocalise into the conduction band \citep{Lany2005anion,Zhang2006systematic}. A formation energy computed in a finite supercell for such a configuration does not describe an isolated neutral defect, which is why Kumagai et al.\ excluded these entries from their machine-learning subset \citep{Kumagai2021insights}. Of the \numScreeningAbsenceReleaseNeutralCompletedEntries{} completed neutral entries of the release, \numScreeningAbsenceReleaseNeutralCompletedPhs{} carry the flag.

The excluded entries are not a random sample of oxide chemistry. The fraction flagged is \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationNbPhsRateWith{} among entries of hosts that contain Nb and \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationWPhsRateWith{} among those that contain W, \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationTiPhsRateWith{} with Ti, \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationVPhsRateWith{} with V, \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationTaPhsRateWith{} with Ta and \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationMoPhsRateWith{} with Mo. These are \dzero{} cations with a low-lying empty $d$ band. For Zr and Hf, also \dzero{} but with a higher-lying $d$ band, the fraction is only \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationZrPhsRateWith{} and \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationHfPhsRateWith{}, and for the $s$-band conductors Zn, Ga, Sn and In it is between \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationGaPhsRateWith{} and \numScreeningAbsencePhsByCationInPhsRateWith{}. The median hybrid-functional band gap of the host is \numScreeningAbsenceMedianHybridGapEVPhs{}~eV for flagged entries against \numScreeningAbsenceMedianHybridGapEVNotPhs{}~eV for the rest. The perovskites SrTiO$_{3}$ and BaTiO$_{3}$ are absent from the universe for this reason, and monoclinic TiO$_{2}$ keeps only one of its oxygen sites.

The scope of every result in this paper follows: the models describe oxides in which the neutral oxygen vacancy is a deep, localised defect. Shallow-donor oxides, chiefly early-transition-metal \dzero{} oxides and other small-gap oxides, are systematically absent from the training data, and a prediction for an oxide whose neutral vacancy would be a shallow donor lies outside the domain of the models. These counts describe the release; they do not establish the electronic mechanism of any individual entry.
